The Process

The idea

I look for things that excite me and that I know less about. It gives me a reason to read, question, and learn along the way. This project started with a simple thought: people usually ask me two questions — “Why don’t you gain weight despite eating so much?” and “Is it Vadodara or Baroda?” I decided to answer the easier one :p

The Data

I started with Census data on cities and towns, and then went down the rabbit hole of researching their names. I looked at prefixes, suffixes, meanings, origins, and eventually the larger patterns they form. There wasn’t a single dataset that brought together everything I wanted to explore, so a large part of the process involved building and compiling the dataset from scratch. Along the way, I also had to account for multiple and sometimes contested explanations for the same name.

The Tools

I used Excel and Python for cleaning, organising, and aggregating the data. Observable helped me build and experiment with the visualisations, while VS Code became the space where everything came together. The final webpage is hosted on GitHub.

The Narrative

I read through articles, historical references, and etymological sources to understand not just what names mean, but why they came to mean what they do. The narrative gradually shifted from simply looking at names to noticing patterns — common endings, recurring themes, and eventually the politics of renaming. I wanted the story to feel less like a database and more like a process of discovering what a name can reveal.

The Design

The visual language developed quite organically. I picked up artefacts that reminded me of cities — old signboards, nameplates, maps, stamps, and the typography of the boards we encounter while travelling. I wanted the interface to feel familiar without becoming overly historical. The palette was kept playful and bright to balance what could otherwise become a very text-heavy subject. The design also evolved alongside the narrative, with each interaction trying to make the act of looking closer at a name feel like part of the story.
