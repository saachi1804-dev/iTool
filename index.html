There's an old Indian saying: "Kos-kos par badle paani, chaar kos par vaani"—the water changes every few miles, and so does the language.While the saying celebrates India's rich linguistic diversity, it also raises a simple everyday question: How easily can we communicate as we travel across the country?

Languages rarely stop where political boundaries begin. Some stretch across state borders, while others remain deeply rooted in a single region. Using India’s Census data on mother tongues, bilingualism, and trilingualism, this article explores just how easy or difficult it might be to strike up a conversation as you travel across the country. Bilingualism refers to a person’s ability to speak two languages, while trilingualism refers to the ability to speak three.

The Census records this alongside a person’s mother tongue two other languages they are fluent in. Note: The Census groups thousands of reported mother tongues into standardized language categories based on linguistic classification and speaker thresholds.
