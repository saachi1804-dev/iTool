Open your mouth in India and you are rolling dice. According to the 2011 census, people gave over 19,500 different answers when asked their mother tongue and other languages they can speak and understand. Most of those answers will never appear on a map, in a textbook, or on a road sign.

India's mother-tongue population of several "regional" languages outnumbers entire national languages elsewhere, anywhere in the world.

Hindi's native-speaker count alone (528 million) is bigger than the entire population of Africa's most populous 40 countries combined.

Sort through the chaos, and you get 121 recognised languages. Only 22 of these are official "Scheduled Languages." The rest — 99 of them — are spoken by real communities, just without the government stamp.

Here's where it gets surprising: that stamp has very little to do with size. Hindi, one of the 22, is spoken by 44% of the country. Sanskrit, also one of the 22, has just 25,000 speakers — fewer than a small town. Meanwhile Bhili/Bhilodi, which isn't on the list at all, is spoken by over 10 million people.
